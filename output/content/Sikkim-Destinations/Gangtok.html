Gangtok: The Hillside Haven

A harmonious blend of ancient Buddhist traditions and 21st-century modernity.

Quick Facts

Area: 954 sq km

Best Time: Feb-May & Sept-Nov
Languages: English, Nepali, Hindi, Tibetan

STD Code: 03592

History of Gangtok

Gangtok, the capital city of Sikkim, is situated 110 kms from Siliguri and is filled with different cultures and religions existing in perfect harmony. The earliest records date back to the construction of the hermitic Gangtok monastery in 1716. It remained a small hamlet until the construction of the Enchey Monastery in 1840 made it a pilgrimage center.

In 1894, Thutob Namgyal shifted the capital from Tumlong to Gangtok, increasing its importance. Following India's independence in 1947, Sikkim became a nation-state with Gangtok as its capital, eventually becoming part of India in 1975.

Cultural Tapestry

The city celebrates Hindu festivals like Diwali and Makar Sankranti alongside Buddhist festivals such as Losar (Tibetan New Year), Saga Dawa, and Lhabab Duechen. Music is a core part of life here, with locals enjoying Western rock, Hindi pop, and indigenous Nepali rock.

Food lovers relish noodle-based dishes like Thukpa, Chowmein, and Wontons. The Momo remains the most popular snack. Sports enthusiasts primarily follow Football and Cricket, with the Paljor Stadium being the city's main sporting ground.

Sightseeing In Gangtok

Rumtek Monastery

The largest monastery in the eastern Himalayas, built by Gyalwa Karmapa strictly according to Tibetan designs, located 24 kms from Gangtok.

Institute of Tibetology

A world-renowned center for research into Mahayana Buddhism, housing rare Tibetan, Sanskrit, and Lepcha manuscripts and over 200 icons.

Enchey Monastery

This 200-year-old monastery was built by Lama Druptop Karpo, a tantric master. It is famous for the 'Chaam' masked dance festival in January.

Tsomgo Lake

A serene lake 40 km from the city at an altitude of 3780m. It freezes during winter and is surrounded by vibrant alpine flowers in spring.

Dud-Dul Chorten

Built in 1945, this Stupa is surrounded by 10 prayer wheels (Mani-Lhaskar) and contains holy books and mantras.

Deer Park (Rustamji Park)

Home to the Red Panda and Himalayan Bear, the park features a massive statue of Lord Buddha and offers mesmerizing views of the Gangtok hills.

Travel Guide

By Air

The nearest airport is Bagdogra (124 km). Regular buses and flights from Delhi are available.

By Rail

Nearest railheads are New Jalpaiguri (125 km) and Siliguri. Mahananda Exp connects Delhi to the region daily.

By Road

Well connected to Darjeeling, Kalimpong, and Siliguri. State transport buses provide regular services.

Don't Miss

  • Nam Nam View Point for Tsomgo forest views
  • Tasuklakhang-The Royal Chapel
  • Local Momos and Thukpa
  • 200-foot TV Tower landmark
