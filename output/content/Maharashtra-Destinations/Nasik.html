Nasik: The Grape City

The Wine Capital of India and one of the holiest pilgrimage sites on the banks of the Godavari.

City Overview

Nasik is located in the Western Ghats on the edge of the Deccan Peninsula. Popularly known as the 'Wine Capital of India', it is one of the four holiest places in India. The city is famous for hosting the Kumbh Mela every 12 years and is deeply tied to the Ramayana, believed to be the place where Lord Rama stayed during his exile.

185 km from Mumbai | 202 km from Pune
Altitude: 225 meters
Best Time: October to February
Marathi, Hindi, English

History

Nasik was once the capital of the powerful Satavahana dynasty and a vital trading port. During the Mughal period, it was known as "Gulshanabad" before returning to its original name under the Peshwas in 1751.

The city played a significant role in India's freedom struggle, hosting movements led by Mahatma Gandhi and Dr. Ambedkar’s temple entry movement in 1932.

Major Attractions

Kumbh Mela

The world's largest religious gathering, occurring here every 12 years. Devotees take a ritual holy dip in the Godavari River to cleanse their souls.

Panchawati

Located in northern Nasik, this site is named after five Banyan trees. It is legendary as the residence of Lord Rama and Sita during their exile.

Sundarnarayan Temple

Built in 1756 by Gangadhar Yashwant Chandrachud, this temple is dedicated to Lord Vishnu and sits near the Ahillyabai Holkar Bridge.

Sita Gumpha

An ancient cave in Panchawati reached by a narrow staircase. It contains idols of Rama, Laxman, and Sita, marking the spot where Sita was reportedly abducted.

Sacred Spots

  • Ramkund

    A holy pond in the Godavari river where Rama is said to have bathed. It is considered the 'Ganges of the South'.

  • Kalaram Temple

    Built in 1794 with unique black stones, this is one of the most significant temples in the Panchawati area.

  • Modakeshwar Ganesh Temple

    Famous for a 'Swayambhu' (self-emerged) idol of Lord Ganesh. The name comes from his favorite sweet, the Modak.

Travel Guide

By Air

The nearest airport is Mumbai International Airport, located 185 km away.

By Rail

A major Central Railway station. The Panchvati Express connects to Mumbai in just 4 hours.

By Road

Well-connected via private and state buses to Mumbai, Thane, and Pune.

Local Festivals

Ganesh Chaturthi, Dussehra, Diwali, and Holi are celebrated with immense gaiety.
