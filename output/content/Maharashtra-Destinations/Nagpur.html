Nagpur: The Orange City

The green heart of India and the historic second capital of Maharashtra.

City Overview

Founded in 1702 by King Bakht Buland Shah of Deogad, Nagpur serves as the second capital of Maharashtra. Named after the River Nag, it is recognized as the second greenest city in India and is globally celebrated for its world-class oranges.

Area: 9892 sq. km
Best Time: Sept to April
Marathi, Hindi, English
STD Code: 0712

Gems of Nagpur

Dheekshabhoomi

A sacred Buddhist pilgrimage site where Dr. B.R. Ambedkar embraced Buddhism in 1956. It is a major holy monument hosting thousands every year on Ashok Vijaya Dashmi.

Ramtek

Historic site where Kalidasa composed "Meghdoot". Home to 600-year-old Ram and Jain temples featuring exquisite carvings and sculptures.

Sevagram

Established by Mahatma Gandhi in 1933. This ashram served as his headquarters for social service programs for 15 years.

Markanda

Situated on the banks of the Vainganga River, this cluster of 24 temples features architecture strongly resembling the Khajuraho temples.

Orange Specialty & Wildlife

The Orange Heritage

Orange cultivation began here in 1896 by Raghujiraje Bhonsle. Today, the Vidarbha region cultivates oranges across 80,000 hectares, producing nearly 5 lakh tons annually. It is one of India's most remunerative foreign exchange-earning crops.

Pench National Park

Located 86 km from Nagpur, this park is also known as Jawaharlal Nehru Udyan. It was built around the Pench Irrigation project and serves as a popular destination for day trips and wildlife viewing.

Travel Guide

By Air

Sonegaon Airport (8 km south) connects to Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Pune, and Calcutta.

By Rail

Nagpur Railway Station is a critical junction on Central and Southeastern lines, connecting to all major metros.

By Road

Well-connected by a robust road network with all industrial centers of the country.
