Jaisalmer: The Golden City

A majestic desert oasis situated in the heart of India's Thar Desert.

Desert Heritage

Named after its founder, Rao Jaisal Yadav, Jaisalmer is famously known as the "Golden City" because the yellow sand grants a golden tinge to the town and its surroundings[cite: 582, 583]. Historically a princely state, it is nestled in the heart of the Thar Desert[cite: 580, 581].

Jaisalmer Fort

Built in 1156 by the Bhati ruler Jaisal on Trikuta hill, this fort is famously known as the "Sonar Kella" (Golden Fort)[cite: 583, 584]. Its lion-colored walls turn into a honey-gold hue during sunset[cite: 586].

Main Attractions:

  • Rajmahal: The royal palace[cite: 585].
  • Temples: Includes Laxminath and various Jain temples[cite: 585].

Architectural Wonders: The Havelis

Patwo ki Haveli

The largest and most attractive haveli in the city[cite: 589]. Built by merchant Guman Chand Patwa and his sons, this five-story complex took 50 years to complete[cite: 590, 591].

Salam Singh ki Haveli

Built in 1815 by the then Prime Minister, it features arched roofs decorated with peacock-formed brackets[cite: 587, 588].

Nathmalji ki Haveli

Ornately decorated with birds, elephants, flowers, and unique modern carvings like a steam engine and bicycle[cite: 593].

Shreenath ji ki Haveli

A 15th-century haveli where the descendants of the original Vyas family still reside, partially operating as a hotel[cite: 594, 595].

Jain Heritage

The city boasts beautiful Jain temples dedicated to Lord Sai Nath (16th Tirthankar) and Lord Parshvanath (23rd Tirthankar)[cite: 596].

Other nearby pilgrimage centers include Amarsagar, Lodavra, Pokharan, and Brahmsar[cite: 597].

City Guide

Transportation

Connected to all major Indian cities via rail and buses[cite: 598]. Local transport relies on autos and buses[cite: 598].

Administration

The city serves as the administrative headquarters for Jaisalmer district[cite: 582].
