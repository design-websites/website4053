Ranthambore National Park

A majestic blend of royal history and untamed wilderness in the heart of Rajasthan.

Historical Legacy

Ranthambore's history is defined by grand battles and royal hunting traditions. Captured by Akbar in 1569 AD, it was established as a game sanctuary in 1955 and declared a Tiger Reserve in 1973. Today, the expanded reserve includes the Sawai Man Singh and Keladevi sanctuaries.

Area: 1,334 sq. km
Best Season: October to March
Rajasthani, Hindi, English
STD Code: 07462

Wildlife & Landmarks

Ranthambore Fort

A 10th-century Chauhan marvel and one of Rajasthan's oldest forts. It features massive walls, huge gates, and ancient temples designed to keep enemies at bay.

Jogi Mahal

A forest rest house overlooking Padam Talab. It is home to an ancient banyan tree, famously the second largest in India.

The Lakes & Valleys

Padam Talab

The park's largest lake, named after seasonal water lilies. An excellent spot for early morning wildlife sightings.

Raj Bagh Talab

The most scenic lake, dotted with ancient ruins and a favorite haunt for Sambhar deer and tigers.

Kachida Valley

A rugged area on the park outskirts known for its high population of Panthers and Sloth Bears.

Malik Talab

The smallest lake, bustling with avian life and home to the Indian Marsh Crocodile.

Biodiversity

With dry deciduous vegetation, the park hosts 300 species of birds and 30 species of mammals.

  • Tigers, Leopards & Hyenas
  • Eagles, Falcons & Kingfishers
  • Pythons, Cobras & Crocodiles
  • Predominant Dhok Plantation
