Dandi: Landmark of Freedom

The historic shore of the Salt Satyagraha and the site of Gandhiji's legendary 241-mile journey.

Introduction

Dandi is situated on the coastline, just 5 kms from the heart of Surat. While it is a key center for salt production, its primary fame stems from its historical importance.

In March 1930, Gandhiji performed the famous ‘Dandi March Salt Satyagraha’ here to protest the salt laws of the British Government. Gandhiji and his 78 followers walked approximately 241 miles from Sabarmati Ashram to this very shore, sparking a movement that paved the way for India's independence.

History & Heritage

During the time of the Dandi March, the village lacked basic infrastructure like roads, water, and electricity. Today, the Government has preserved the bungalow where Gandhiji stayed, converting it into an exhibition of Dandi March photographs.

The Prayer Monument

A monument stands under a banyan tree where Gandhiji held multi-religious prayer meetings. It was inaugurated by Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru in 1961.

Hajani Bibi ni Dargah

A significant holy site for the Dawoodi Bohra community who visit Dandi for pilgrimage.

Environment & Salt Production

Dandi sits on the Arabian Sea shore at the coast of the Purna River. Historically, high tides would surround the village like an island, leaving water trapped in low-lying lands to evaporate and produce salt naturally.

However, the same tides would often flood houses and wash away agricultural land. To protect the village, a Soil Dam was constructed to block the water, which ultimately stopped the natural production of salt.

Modern Dandi has evolved into a well-equipped village with electricity, schools, hostels, and tap water in every house.

Etymology & Demographics

What's in a Name?

  • Diva Dandi: Referring to an old lighthouse that once stood on the shore.
  • Dando: A Gujarati term for land covered by water, reflecting Dandi's island-like geography.

Population

The village currently has a population of about 1260. During the historic movement in 1930, the population was estimated to be only 460 people.
