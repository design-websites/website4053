Bodhgaya: The Land of Enlightenment

The sacred ground where Gautam Buddha attained supreme knowledge and became the Awakened One.

Overview

BodhGaya is an important pilgrimage site in the history of Buddhism as it is believed to be the place of Buddha's enlightenment attainment. Located in the Gaya district of Bihar, the pilgrim site is primarily associated with the Mahabodhi temple complex.

Its earlier name was Bodhimanda, which means the ground under the bodhi tree. It was also known as Uruvela, Vajrasana, Mahabodhi, and Sambodhi. Alongside Lumbini, Sarnath, and Kushinagar, it ranks as one of the four most important Buddhist sites in the world.

In 2002, the Mahabodhi temple of BodhGaya was designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History of BodhGaya

In 500 BC, Gautam Siddhartha, son of King Suddodhana, arrived here wandering in search of knowledge. On the banks of the Falgu river, he sat under a tree and meditated for three days and three nights, after which he attained enlightenment and found the answers he was searching for.

Seven weeks after this event, Buddha reached Sarnath to begin teaching. Centuries later, the Mauryan emperor Ashoka visited Bodhgaya and built a temple under the Bodhi tree in the 3rd century BC. Inside this temple, an enlightenment throne decorated with Kushana and Huvishka coins was discovered.

With the decline of Buddhism, the temple suffered and was eventually buried under sand and soil. In 1833, Sir Alexander Cunningham of the Asiatic Society rediscovered and restored it, returning the glory to this sacred site.

Festivals & Celebrations

The most significant celebration in Bodhgaya is Vaishakh Poornima (also called Buddha Poornima), held during April-May. It is a tradition to visit Bodhgaya on this full moon day to celebrate the anniversary of Buddha's enlightenment.

Travel & Transport

By Road

Bihar State Tourism Development Corporation provides full travel facilities from Patna to visit the entire Buddha circuit. Direct bus services are also available from various cities to Gaya.

By Rail

Trains are available from most Indian cities to Gaya. Bodhgaya is approximately 5 km from Gaya.

By Air

Bodhgaya airport is located 5 km from Gaya, providing convenient access for air travelers.

Local Transport

Internal transport within the district depends largely on auto-rickshaws and personal vehicles.
