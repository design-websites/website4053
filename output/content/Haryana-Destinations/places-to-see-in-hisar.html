Hisar: The Fortress of Feroz

An ancient crossroads of Harappan civilization and Tughlaq architectural grandeur.

Location and History

Formerly spelled as Hissar, this city in Haryana was founded by the ruler Firoze Shah Tughlaq. The name itself means "fortress" in Turkish, and it was originally known as Hissar-e-Firoza (the Fort of Feroz).

Connected with ancient Vedic tribes
Main cotton industry hub of India
Home to Hissar Agricultural Institute
162 km from Delhi

Architectural Landmarks

Palace of Feroze Shah

A remarkable complex featuring an underground chamber and a mosque. The site also borders the ruins of the Harappan civilization, blending multiple eras of history.

Lat Ki Masjid

An outstanding L-shaped mosque representing the Suljuk style with Hindu grid influences. It is famous for its unique lithic pillar and square chamber.

Gujri Mahal

Built from stone, this palace is now in ruins, but its raised platform used for ancient social gatherings remains in good condition.

Jahaz Kothi

A magnificent palace with a Mughlai touch, built for an Irish ruler known as Jahaz Sahib who requested the structure from Mughal rulers.

Ancient Heritage

Hisar is a significant site for the Harappan Civilization, with prominent ruins found at three specific mounds:

  • Banavali: Shows the ruins of an ancient township.
  • Kunal: Displays three distinct stages of civilization development.
  • Agroha: Revealed the discovery of coins dating back to the 3rd Century B.C.

Sacred Sites

  • Jain Pillar

    Located just outside the fort, this 2-foot pillar with a diamond symbol on top is regarded as highly sacred by the Jain community.

  • Tomb of Pranpir Badshah

    The resting place of the great Sufi saint, situated uniquely inside the premises of the Government College of Hisar.
