A Tour to Sirsa

Tracing the ancient roots of 'Sairishaka' in the western reaches of Haryana.

Historical Legacy

Sirsa is the westernmost town of Haryana, known in ancient times as Sairishaka as mentioned in the Mahabharata. Founded in the 7th century AD by King 'Saras' who built its original fort, the town has also been known historically as Sarsuti and Sirsapattan.

Known for Cotton & Citrus production
323 Villages across 4 Tehsils
Birthplace of Lt. Devi Lal Chaudhary
Ancient fort ruins from 7th Century AD

Places of Interest

Archaeological Mounds

  • Sikanderpur: Home to stone sculptures of Lord Shiva and Indra, plus Rang Mahal pottery.
  • Arnian Wali: A 10-foot high mound containing ancient pottery remains.
  • Suchan & Mangiana: Sites featuring medieval wares and ancient archaeological specimens.

Spiritual Landmarks

  • Dera Sacha Sauda: Built in 1948 by Saint Shah Mastana, featuring a massive hall and 600 rooms.
  • Dera Baba Sarsai Nath: A historic temple visited by Emperor Shah Jahan for his son's health.
  • Saint Baba Bihari Samadhi: A peaceful samadhi located within a beautiful western garden.

Historical Graves & Temples

Tomb of Khawaja Pir

Dedicated to Khawaja Abdul Shankar, who arrived with Mohammed Ghori. This site is also significant as it is said Guru Nanak Dev resided here for 40 days.

Dera Baba Sarsai Nath Temple

Houses temples dedicated to Shiva and Durga. The dome and land were donated by Emperor Shah Jahan as an offering for his son's blessings.

Travel Info

By Road

Extensive bus facilities connect Sirsa to major cities in the region.

By Rail

The nearest major station is Ramgondam, located 66 km from the city.

By Air

The nearest major airport is Hyderabad (268 km), well-connected to India's major cities.
