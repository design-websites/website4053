Kurukshetra: Land of Gita

The legendary battlefield of the Mahabharata and a cornerstone of Indian spirituality.

Mythological & Historical Significance

Kurukshetra is famously known as Dharmakshetra, the site of the 18-day Mahabharata battle between the Kauravas and Pandavas. It is the sacred place where Lord Krishna delivered the Karma theory to Arjuna, known today as the holy Bhagavad Gita.

Named after King Kuru
Home to the Holy Gita Theory
Part of Emperor Harsha's Golden Age
Sacred to all ten Sikh Gurus

Major Attractions

Religious Landmarks

  • Jyotisar: The exact spot where the Gita was delivered.
  • Brahmsarovar: A vast, holy water tank for pilgrims.
  • Sannihit Tank: Believed to be the meeting point of seven holy Saraswatis.
  • Bhishm Kund & Baan-Ganga: Sites tied directly to epic battle events.

Culture & Heritage

  • Sri Krishna Museum: Artifacts depicting the life of Lord Krishna.
  • Gurudwara 6th Pathshahi: Commemorating visits from Sikh Gurus.
  • Tomb of Sheikh Chaheli: A stunning Sufi saint memorial.
  • Sthaneshvar Mahadev: An ancient temple of great religious power.

Archaeological Interest

The region is a treasure trove of antiquarian interests, including Painted Greyware pottery found at sites like Asthipura, Raja Karan ka Tila, Bhagpura, and Bhor Saidan.

Modern Kurukshetra

The city is home to Kurukshetra University, a world-class Science Museum, and thriving commercial mandies, blending ancient heritage with modern progress.

Geography & Climate

Spanning 1,682 sq km, the district lies in the plains of the Saraswati, Ghaggar, and Markanda rivers. This location provides rich alluvial soil to the region.

Summer: Hot (~45°C)
Winter: Cold (~3°C)

Travel Guide

By Rail

Well-connected with major Indian cities through a robust railway network.

By Road

Excellent road transportation links the district to the rest of Haryana and neighboring states.

Administration

Separated from Karnal in 1973; includes sub-states Pehowa and Thanesar.
