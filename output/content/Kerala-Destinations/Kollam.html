Kollam: The Ancient Emporium

A historic seaport on the banks of Ashtamudi Lake, where commerce and nature meet.

Quick Facts

Area: 2579 sq. km
Altitude: Sea level
Best Time: August to March
Annual Rainfall: 278 cm
Climate: Tropical
Aliases: Desinganadu, Quilon

History & Heritage

Kollam has maintained a commercial reputation since the days of the Phoenicians and Romans. Famed traveler Ibn Batuta regarded it as one of the five main ports for Chinese trade. Its history is a rich tapestry of international relations, having hosted Portuguese, Dutch, and British trading centers over the centuries.

The Malayalam era is historically calculated from the founding of Kollam in the 9th century. Under the rule of Velu Thampi Dalawa, the town modernized with new bazaars, solidifying its status as a prosperous commercial and industrial center.

Major Attractions

Kollam Town

Built in the 9th Century by Syrian merchant Sapir Iso, this town is the headquarters of the world's cashew trade. It edges the famous Ashtamudi Lake and serves as a gateway to Kerala's backwaters.

Palaruvi Waterfalls

Meaning 'Stream of Milk,' this is one of Kerala's most beautiful falls. Located near Aryankavu on the NH 208, the best season to visit is from June to January.

Thenmala Eco-Tourism

India's first planned eco-tourism destination. Surrounded by dense forest, it features rubber and tea plantations, a dam site, and timber exports.

Oachira Pilgrim Center

A unique center where there is no temple building or idol; people worship the 'Parabrahmam.' Famous for the Oachirakkali festival and screw pine mat handicrafts.

Spiritual & Forest Ranges

Achencoil

A pilgrim center in dense forest housing the ancient Sastha temple. Known for the most lavish flower offerings (Pushpabhishekam) in South India.

Kulathupuzha

An important forest range well-known for its elephant population and supplying raw materials for paper manufacture.

Mayyanad

Noted for nine shrines and temples, most notably the Subramanian temple at Umayanallor, said to be consecrated by Shri Sankaracharya.

Travel Guide

By Air

The nearest airport is Thiruvananthapuram, located approximately 72 km from Kollam.

By Rail

Kollam is a major railway junction connected to all important cities in Kerala and the rest of India.

By Road

Well-connected via the Kollam-Alappuzha National Highway and other major state routes.

Local Transport

KSRTC buses, private taxis, and auto-rickshaws are readily available for city travel.
