Calicut: The City of Spices

A historic coastal gem where world explorers first set foot on Indian soil.

Overview

Located on the Malabar Coast in Northern Kerala, Calicut (Kozhikode) is a beautiful city renowned for its beaches, backwaters, and historical significance.

The city's backwaters comprise a self-supporting ecosystem of lakes, canals, and estuaries. Formed by 44 rivers draining into the Arabian Sea, this network is unique to Kerala and serves as a vital local transport link between villages.

Historical Landmarks

Kappakkadavu Beach

Also known as Kappad Beach, this is where Vasco da Gama first set foot in India on May 27, 1498. A small stone monument commemorates this world-altering event.

The Thali Temple

An ancient Shiva temple dating back to the Zamorin rule. Though destroyed by Tipu Sultan and Hyder Ali, it was renovated in the 18th century and houses shrines for Ganapati and Narasimha.

Pazhassiraja Museum

Run by the State Archaeological Department, it houses ancient coins, mural paintings, and bronzes. An adjacent art gallery features the works of Raja Ravi Varma.

Mananchira

Originally the palace tank of King Mana Vikrama, this area has been transformed into a splendor of traditional architecture with green lawns and a musical fountain.

Nature & Science

Kadalundi Bird Sanctuary

A haven for migratory birds spread over 3 km. Home to 100 resident species and 60 migratory species, including terns, gulls, and herons that visit from November to April.

Dolphin's Point

A scenic spot where early morning visitors can spot dolphins playing in the sea. The beach also features a lighthouse and the Calicut Town Center.

Science Planetarium

Located in Jaffarkhan Colony, this complex features scientific gadgets, puzzles, and a planetarium to unravel the mysteries of the stars and planets.
