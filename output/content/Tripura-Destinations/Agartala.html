History & Overview

Agartala, the capital of Tripura, is a storehouse of rich cultural diversity situated in North Eastern India. Once part of a Hindu kingdom, it was taken over by the Mughals in 1733 and later came under British rule in 1808. Following India's independence, Tripura became a state in 1972, with Agartala as its capital.

The city lies on the Haora River and is uniquely located just 2 km from the Bangladesh border. It is renowned for its modern twin palaces, Ujjayanta and Kunjaban.

Top Sightseeing Spots

Ujjayanta Palace

A stunning Indo-Saracenic mansion built in 1901 by Maharaja Radhakishore Manikya, set within 28 hectares of parkland.

Kunjaban Palace

Built in 1917, this hillock palace serves as the official residence of the Governor of Tripura.

Neermahal

Located 55km away, this unique "Water Palace" sits in the middle of Rudrasagar Lake, blending Hindu and Mughal styles.

Unakoti

A historical pilgrimage site featuring massive 8th-century rock-cut carvings and a 30-foot statue of Lord Shiva.

Exploring Around Agartala

Beyond the city, travelers can visit Debtamura to see 15th-century rock carvings of Hindu deities on the banks of the Gomati River, or explore Pilak, famous for its terracotta plaques and mammoth stone images dating back to the 8th century.

How to Reach
