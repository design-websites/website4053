Guwahati: The Light of the East

The bustling gateway to the exotic north-eastern regions of India.

History & Overview

Guwahati, the largest city in Assam, sits majestically on the banks of the Brahmaputra. Its name is derived from "Guwa" (areca nut) and "Hat" (market).

Once known as 'Pragiyotishpura', it was the capital of Kamrup kings. Today, it serves as a vital service center for the oil and tea industries, hosting India's largest tea auction market.

The city is a tapestry of ancient Hindu temples, with the Kamakhya temple serving as the heartbeat of Assamese life and culture.

Major Attractions

Kamakhya Temple

Perched atop Nilachal hill, this 10th-century shrine is the most revered seat of Shakti worship and Tantricism in the world.

Navagraha Temple

The "Temple of Nine Planets" is an ancient seat of astrology. Its red beehive dome houses lingams representing celestial bodies like the Sun, Moon, and Mars.

Umananda Shiva Temple

This peaceful shrine stands on a small island in the middle of the Brahmaputra, accessible via motor boats and ferries from Umananda Ghat.

Vashistha Ashram

The abode of sage Vashistha is a lush retreat 12 km from the station, featuring the confluence of three streams: Lalita, Kanta, and Sandhya.

Nature & Spirituality

Guwahati Zoo

Explore the largest natural zoo in the country, showcasing the diverse wildlife of the North East.

Bhubaneshwari Temple

Located above Kamakhya, this temple provides a spectacular bird's-eye view of the city and the river below.

Ugratara Temple

Famous for its golden idol, this city shrine is known for its traditional rituals and buffalo sacrifices.

Travel Info

By Air

The international airport connects Guwahati to major Indian cities and international hubs like Bangkok.

By Rail

The main railhead of Assam, offering direct links to New Delhi, Kolkata, Mumbai, and Bangalore.

By Road

A well-built road network with regular state transport buses connecting all major tourist destinations.

North Guwahati

Accessed via the Saraighat Bridge or ferries, North Guwahati is almost a separate town across the Brahmaputra.
