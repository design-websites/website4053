Manas Tiger Reserve

A UNESCO World Heritage Site nestled in the foothills of the Himalayas.

Reserve Profile

Location: 176 km from Guwahati, Assam
Area: 391 sq. kms (Core) / 2840 sq. kms (Reserve)
Best Time: October to April
Climate: Min 2°C, Max 32°C

Historical Background

Manas is located along the Indo-Bhutan border with contiguous wildlife habitats in Bhutan. Originally, the area served as the hunting grounds for the Raja of Gauripur and the Cooch-Behar royal family.

The Manas Sanctuary was established in 1928, and the area was later declared a National Park in 1990. It remains the only Project Tiger site in Assam.

A Unique Biosphere

The Majestic Tiger

While Kaziranga is known for the Rhino, Manas is famous for its tigers. The riparian forests provide by far the best habitat for tigers, hosting a very high concentration of the species.

Rare & Endangered Species

Home to twenty endangered species listed in the IUCN Red Data Book, including the rare Golden Langur, Hispid Hare, Pigmy Hog, and occasional sightings of the Red Panda.

Ecosystem & Habitat

The park's terrain consists of roughly 45% grassland and 55% tree land. The Manas River, which demarcates the India-Bhutan border, creates a dynamic environment of erosion and accretion, sustaining very high productivity.

Forest Dwellers

  • Elephant & Rhino
  • Water Buffalo
  • Hoolock Gibbon
  • Capped Langur

Avian Diversity

  • Giant Hornbills
  • Scarlet Minivet
  • Brahminy Ducks
  • Pelicans & Herons

Plan Your Trip

By Air

Guwahati International Airport is the nearest air hub, located 176 km from the reserve.

By Rail

The nearest town and railhead is Barpeta Road, which is 41 km away from the park entrance.

By Road

Manas is well connected by a road network to major towns in Assam and other Indian cities.

Conservation Note

Manas is a highly sensitive ecological zone. Visitors are encouraged to observe wildlife from a distance and respect the natural habitat of the endangered species.
