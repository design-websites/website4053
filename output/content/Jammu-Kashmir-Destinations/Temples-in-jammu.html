Jammu: City of Temples

A holy and peaceful city situated in the foothills of the Himalaya mountain range.

The Legend of Raja Jambu Lochan

Jammu was named after its discoverer, King Raja Jambu Lochan, following a remarkable hunting trip.

The King witnessed a tiger and a goat drinking water from the same spot in the Tawi River. Surprised by this peaceful sight, he decided to build a city where no enmity existed between living creatures. This vision led to the establishment of innumerable temples, defining Jammu today as the "City of Temples."

Renowned Shrines

Raghunath Mandir

Located in the heart of the city, this complex was built in 1857 and is considered the pride of the region. It is the largest temple complex in all of North India.

  • Interiors: Plated with gold.
  • Library: Features rare Sanskrit manuscripts and texts.
  • Deities: Dedicated to Lord Vishnu and Lord Rama.
  • Architecture: Shows strong Mughal influence.

Bawely Mata Mandir (Mahakali Mandir)

Situated inside the Bahu Fort, this temple is dedicated to Mahamaya, the local deity of the Dogras. Built after the coronation of Maharaja Gulab Singh in 1822, the goddess is considered second only to Mata Vaishno Devi.

Special pilgrim attraction on Tuesdays and Sundays.

Shrines of Lord Shiva

Ranbireshwar Temple

Houses 12 crystal Shivalingams ranging from 12" to 18" in size, with thousands of saligrams in the galleries.

Panchbakhtar & Peer Kho

Ancient temples dedicated to Lord Shiva situated alongside the river.

World-Renowned Pilgrimages

Vaishno Devi

Attracts thousands of pilgrims every year. A visit to Bhairav Ghati is required to complete the pilgrimage.

Amarnath Temple

The cave home of Lord Shiva, featuring shrines for Mahakali, Mahalaxmi, and Maha Saraswati. A 13km track from Katra provides access via ponies, porters, or dandies.
