Ladakh Tour

Land of High Passes, Ancient Monasteries, and Eternal Snow.

The Land and People

Ladakh is the northernmost region of Jammu and Kashmir, a breathtaking valley of huge mountains and roads covered in snow. The people here are known for their simplicity and descend from Indo-Aryan and Tibetan origins.

The region consists of two districts: Leh, the largest district spanning 45,110 Sq Km, and Kargil, which became globally recognized following Operation Vijay in 1999.

Geography & Climate

Boundaries: Bounded by the Himalayas, Karakoram, Ladakh, and Zanskar ranges.

Altitude: Approximately 9,000 feet above sea level.

Summer Max: 27°C

Winter Min: -20°C

Monasteries (Gompas)

Ladakh is famous for its aesthetically designed Buddhist monasteries situated on isolated hillocks. They are characterized by mane walls (votive stones with inscribed prayers) and Chortens (semi-religious shrines).

Major Monasteries:

Hemis, Thiksay, Alchi, Lamayuru, Spituk, Phyang, Rizong, Likir, Stakna, Chemday, Takthok, and Matho monastery.

Palaces and Citadels

Leh Palace

Known as "Lhachen Palkhar," this nine-storied structure was built in 1600. It offers panoramic views of Leh and is currently being refurbished by the Archaeological Survey of India.

Stok Palace

The 19th-century home of the royal family. Its museum showcases 400-year-old thangkas, crown jewels, royal dresses, and religious objects.

Shey Palace & Monastery

A former summer retreat built in the 17th century. It houses a massive copper-gilded statue of Buddha Shakyamuni.

Basgo Rabtan

A hilltop castle featuring impressive statues of the Maitreya Buddha—one two stories high made of copper and another three stories high made of clay.

Wildlife & Adventure

Unique Wildlife

Ladakh is home to rare species including gazelles, ibex, ammon (wild sheep), and shapo.

Trekking

The rugged mountainscape provides numerous interesting routes for trekkers to experience sheer natural beauty closely.
