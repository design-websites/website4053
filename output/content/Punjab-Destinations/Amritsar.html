Amritsar

The spiritual heart of Punjab and a gateway to the braveness of Sikh history.

City Snapshot

Area: 7069 sq.km
Altitude: 3500m
Best Time: October to March
Rainfall: 2954 mm
Languages: Punjabi and English
STD Code: 0183

Overview & History

Amritsar is an essential city for the Sikh religion, famous as the home of the Golden Temple. The city's name is derived from the sacred pool within the temple complex.

Founded in 1574 by Guru Ram Das Sahib, the city began as a residence and a sarovar (tank) built in 1577. The sarovar was later renovated in 1586 by Guru Arjun Singh, leading to the name Amritsar.

Amritsar also holds a solemn place in history due to the Jallianwala Bagh tragedy of 1919, a brutal act of firing under the command of General Dyer that resulted in hundreds of deaths.

Major Sightseeing

Golden Temple (Sri Harmandir Sahib)

One of the holiest sites for Sikhs, the Golden Temple exhibits architectural brilliance with influences from both Hindu and Muslim styles.

  • Layed by Guru Ram Das in the 14th century.
  • Features four doorways and gorgeously decorated arches.
  • Standing in the middle of a tank, it welcomes people of all religions.

Jallianwala Bagh

A memorial site near the Golden Temple that bears testimony to the massacre on 13 April 1919. During Baisakhi celebrations, a peaceful congregation was fired upon under the orders of General Dyer.

Wagah Border

The international border between India and Pakistan. Every evening at sunset, soldiers from both sides perform the "Beating the Retreat" ceremony, a synchronized drill involving the lowering of national flags.

Other Points of Interest

Durgiana Mandir

Built in the 16th century and modeled after the Golden Temple, this shrine is dedicated to Goddess Durga, with prayers also offered to Lakshmi and Narayana.

Rambagh Garden

A new-age garden housing Maharaja Ranjit Singh's Palace, which now serves as a museum.

Tarn Taran

Located 25km away, it houses a sacred tank with believed healing powers and a Gurudwara built by Guru Arjan Dev in 1768.

Ram Tirath Temple

Situated on the Amritsar-Chogawan road, it is believed to be the place where Valmiki composed the Ramayana.

Travel Information

By Air

Rajasansi Airport is well linked by flights across India and was recently elevated to international status.

By Rail

Direct train links from Delhi to Amritsar; the journey takes approximately 8-10 hours.

Major Festivals

Baisakhi and Guru Nanak Jayanti are the most significant celebrations in the city.
