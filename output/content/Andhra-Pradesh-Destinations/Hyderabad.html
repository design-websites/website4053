History of Hyderabad

Hyderabad's history began with the establishment of the Qutub Shahi dynasty in 1512, starting with the fortress city of Golconda. Later, the city was redesigned around the Charminar to combat plague and cholera.

Following the fall of Golconda to Aurangazeb in 1687, the city eventually saw the rise of Asaf Jah-I, who proclaimed himself the Nizam. The dynasty ruled until 1948.

The twin city of Secunderabad was born in 1798 as a British cantonment and named after Nizam Sikander Jah. Today, the two cities have merged into a single sprawling metropolitan area.

The Pearl Culture

Despite being far from the coast, Hyderabad is synonymous with luminescent pearls. This is due to historical royal patronage that attracted craftsmen from the Arabian Gulf.

Visit Patther Gatti, an entire street dedicated to pearl shops, to witness this glittering legacy of the Nizams.

Iconic Sightseeing

Golconda Fort

A majestic ruins that once housed the famous Kohinoor diamond. The fort's walls feature masonry blocks weighing up to 7 tons and a circumference of nearly 7km.

Charminar

The principal landmark of the city, built in 1591 to commemorate the end of an epidemic. The four arches face the cardinal points and are illuminated at night.

Salar Jung Museum

One of the world's largest private collections, compiled by Salar Jung III. It features over 35,000 items, including Persian miniatures and weaponry.

Hussain Sagar

A massive lake (Tank Bund) featuring the world's largest stone statue of Buddha, weighing 350 tons. It is now a hub for water sports and recreation.

Historic & Spiritual Sites

Mecca Masjid

Adjacent to Charminar, this is one of the world's largest mosques. Its name comes from bricks made of soil brought specifically from Mecca.

Qutub Shahi & Paigah Tombs

The cemetery of the Qutub Shahi kings features graceful domed tombs, while the Paigah tombs showcase Persian and Rajasthani inlay works.

Osmania University

Founded in 1919, its Arts College building is a masterpiece that blends European and Indian architectural styles.
