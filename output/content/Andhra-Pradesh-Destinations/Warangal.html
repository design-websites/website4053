Warangal: The Heritage City

Step into the ancient capital of the Kakatiya dynasty, a land of stone marvels and rich history.

At a Glance

Area: 12,846 km²
Best Time: November to February
Languages: Telugu, Urdu
Climate: Tropical (Max 39.6°C)
Rainfall: 82.5 cm (Annual)
Old Names: Orugallu, Ekasilanagaram

History & Heritage

Warangal was the ancient capital of the Kakatiyas, an Andhra dynasty that reached its zenith in the 12th century AD. The city's name is derived from "Orugallu" or "Omtikonda," referring to a massive boulder-like hillock located near the Swayambhu Siva Temple.

Today, Warangal is a flourishing commercial and industrial center, yet it remains deeply rooted in its past, standing out for its beautiful lakes, intricate temples, and abundance of antiques and relics.

Major Attractions

Thousand Pillar Temple

Built by Rudra Deva in 1163 AD, this star-shaped temple is a masterpiece of Kakatiya architecture. It features 3 shrines for Shiva, Vishnu, and Surya, and a magnificent polished basalt Nandi.

Warangal Fort

Dating back to the 13th century, built by Ganapati Deva and Rani Rudramma. Though ravaged, its four massive stone gateways and exquisite sculptures still showcase ancient glory.

Bhadra Kali Temple

Located on a hilltop, this temple is famous for its stone image of Goddess Kali depicted with eight arms, each carrying a weapon.

Ramappa Temple

Also known as Ramalingeswara temple, located in Palampet. This 1213 AD monument is a magnificent symbol of the Kakatiya kingdom's richness.

Excursions & More

Kolanupaka (80 km)

A historical religious hub for Jains and Shaivites. It was an alternate capital for the Kalyani Chalukyas and houses a Jain temple with a 5ft Jade image of Mahavira.

Pakhal Lake (50 km)

A man-made lake completed in 1213 AD. It spreads over 30 sq km and was created by harnessing a tributary of the Krishna River.

Sri Veeranarayana Temple

A Chalukyan style temple from 1104 AD, originally built as a Jain shrine and later converted to a Vaishnava temple.

Travel Guide

By Air

Nearest airport is Hyderabad (140 km), which is well-connected to all major Indian metro cities.

By Rail

Warangal lies on the Chennai-Kazipet-Delhi rail route and is linked to all important Indian cities.

By Road

Regular APSRTC bus services from Hyderabad, Vijayawada, Bangalore, and Mysore.

Local Transport

Public transport and coaches are available from the Hanamakonda bus depot.
