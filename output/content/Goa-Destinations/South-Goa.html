South Goa

A serene sanctuary of pristine beaches and rich colonial heritage.

District Overview

The southern district of Goa, covering 1,966 sq km, is renowned for its beautiful and pristine beaches. The sheer allure of its coastline is a major draw for visitors seeking tranquility.

South Goa is geographically divided into three natural regions: the Low lands (coastal line), the Plateaus, and the Mountain region. It is rich in natural resources, particularly iron and manganese ore, which are exported globally to countries like Japan, South Korea, and various European nations.

Margao: The Cultural Heart

Margao is South Goa's district headquarters and the commercial metropolis of Salcete taluka. As Goa's second-largest city, it retains much of its Portuguese colonial past. The name is believed to be a corruption of "Mathgram," referencing the ancient Hindu religious centers (maths) that once flourished here.

The town serves as a perfect example of the smooth intermingling of Portuguese and Hindu cultures. Despite long periods of colonization, traditional Hindu customs remain deeply rooted, and the city remains a significant cultural and commercial hub surrounded by fertile farmland.

Natural Divisions & Tourism

Coastal Low Lands

The 110 km long coastline features a stunning stretch of beaches including Bogmalo, Velsao, Majorda, Colva, Benaulim, Betul, Agonda, and the famous Palolem Beach.

Plateau Lands

Located between the mountains and the coast, these lands range from 30 to 100 meters in height. They are abundant in laterite stone, which is essential for traditional local house construction.

The Mountain Region

The eastern part of South Goa is dominated by the Sahyadri Mountains, which are covered in dense forest. This region features several steep peaks and important natural landmarks.

Tradition and Festivals

Festivals in South Goa are vibrant celebrations shared by all citizens. Ganesh Chaturthi stands as the premier festival of the region, marked by the return of most Goans to their ancestral houses to celebrate with extended family.

Traditional customs have retained a unique Goan character. Additionally, the region’s rivers like the Mandovi and Zuari are vital waterways, historically using ferry boats for transport and currently used to transport ore to the Mormugao harbour.

Must-See Places

Top Beaches

Colva Beach and Palolem Beach are the quintessential South Goa coastal experiences.

Historical Interest

Explore the Arvalem caves and the colonial architecture found throughout Margao.

Waterways

Witness the transport of ore via the Mandovi and Zuari rivers toward Mormugao harbour.
