Bhubaneshwar

The "City of Temples" and the soul of Kalinga architecture.

Background

Bhubaneshwar is the largest city and capital of Odisha. With a history spanning over 2000 years, it was first noted in historical records after the Kalinga war at Dhauli in the 3rd century BC.

Designed in 1946 by German architect Otto Konigsberger, it stands as one of modern India's first planned cities. It forms the ‘Swarna Tribhuja’ (Golden Triangle) along with Puri and Konark, making it one of East India's most visited destinations.

The city is famous for its unique Kalinga architecture, boasting more than 600 ancient temples known for their intricate sculptures.

Major Attractions

Lingaraj Temple

Built in the 11th century, this massive 147-ft structure features the unique Swayambhu Linga—a sacred merge representing half Lord Shiva and half Lord Vishnu.

Mukteswara Temple

Dating to the 10th century, it is renowned for its magnificent stone arch and lavish sculptures reflecting Buddhist cultural influences.

Raja Rani Temple

Known as the ‘love temple,’ this 11th-century delight is covered in carvings of women and couples, surrounded by beautifully maintained gardens.

Parasurameswara Temple

A 7th-century cluster of 20 small temples dedicated to Lord Shiva, featuring ancient wall carvings of animals, birds, and flowers.

Heritage Sites

  • Nandan Kanan

    A premier Zoo and Botanical Garden located 20 km from the city center.

  • Odisha State Museum

    Established in 1948, it houses a vast collection of palm-leaf manuscripts, ancient tools, coins, and traditional musical instruments.

  • Other Notable Sites

    Explore the Udayagiri & Khandagiri Caves, Bindusagar tank, Dhauli hill, and the nearby Chilika Lake.

How to Reach

By Air

Biju Patnaik Airport is 5 km from the city, connecting to Delhi and Mumbai.

By Rail

Bhubaneshwar Railway Station is 6 km from the city center, well-connected to Delhi, Hyderabad, and Bangalore.

By Road

The main bus stand is 6 km from the city, offering regular services to all major regional cities.
