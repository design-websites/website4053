The Legendary Land of the Gallant Queen, Maharani Laxmi Bai.

Overview of Jhansi

Jhansi is a prominent district in Uttar Pradesh situated in Northern India. Spanning an area of 5,024 sq KM with five tehsils and eight blocks, it holds a significant position in the state's heritage. The city is globally recognized for the heroic legacy of Maharani Laxmi Bai, who valiantly led her troops during the British reign.

In modern times, Jhansi has grown as an infrastructure hub, being the intersection of the North-South and East-West corridors. This strategic location has spurred real estate growth and the planning of a new Greenfield airport.

Historical Legacy

Originally known as Balwant Nagar under the Chandela kings, the region regained prominence in 1613 when Raja Bir Singh Deo of Orchha constructed the Fort of Jhansi.

Following a victory against Mohammad Khan Bangash in 1729, the Maratha Peshva Bajirao I was gifted a portion of the Bundela state, making Jhansi a Maratha Suba. It later became the center of the 1857 revolt when the British doctrine of lapse forced Rani Laxmi Bai into a legendary resistance for independence.

Major Attractions

The Fort of Jhansi

Constructed on a rocky hill, this massive fort features ten gates and a wall that historically surrounded the entire city.

Government Museum

Houses an extensive collection of sculptures, paintings, and 1857 mutiny memorials made from metal scrap.

Shrine of St. Jude

An important Catholic landmark where the bones of St. Jude are buried. It hosts a week-long festival every October.

Modern Infrastructure

Visit the cross-section of India's major corridors, showcasing the rapid urban development of the district.
