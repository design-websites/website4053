Allahabad Tour

Welcome to the King of Tirthas, where dimensions of history and holiness meet.

The Holy Sangam

Allahabad is synonymous with Sangam or Prayag, meaning "place of sacrifice" and "confluence". The city is uniquely situated on the confluence of the Ganges, Yamuna, and the mythical river Saraswati. Hindu mythology states that Lord Brahma, the creator god, chose this land for Prakrishta Yajna.

Site of Annual Magha Mela
One of the holiest cities in India

The Legend of Kumbh Mela

According to legend, Vishnu was carrying a kumbh (pot) of amrita (nectar) when a scuffle broke out between the gods, and four drops were spilled. They fell to earth at the four tirthas: Prayag, Haridwar, Nasik, and Ujjain.

This event is commemorated every three years by the Kumbh Mela. The Sangam is known as Tirtharaja, the "King of Tirthas", and its Mela, held once every twelve years, is recognized as the greatest and holiest of all.

Allahabad Fort

Once among the best garrisons in the Mughal Empire. It houses the Ashokan Pillar (232 BC), Saraswati Kup, and the Jodhabai Palace. A limited area remains open for visitors.

Anand Bhawan

The ancestral home of Jawaharlal Nehru and Indira Gandhi. This historic residence of the Nehru family has been converted into a fine museum.

Hanuman Mandir

A unique temple at the Sangam, it is the only one in North India where the idol of Lord Hanuman is seen in a reclining posture.

Patalpuri Temple

An underground temple inside the fort housing the Akshaya Vat (immortal tree). Visited by the Chinese traveler Hiuen Tsang.

Historic Parks & Gardens

  • Alfred Park: The largest park in Allahabad, holding significant historical value.

  • Minto Park: Now known as Madan Mohan Malaviya Park, a prominent landmark in the city's chronicles.

  • Khusrau Bagh: Houses the tomb of Prince Khusru, son of Emperor Jahangir.

At a Glance

Shankar Viman Mandapam

A 130-feet high structure featuring four distinct floors.

Ashoka Pillar

Standing 10.6 meters high, it features Persian inscriptions of Emperor Jahangir.

Travel Tip
