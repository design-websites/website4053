Lucknow: The Golden City

A timeless tale of Nawabi culture, architectural grandeur, and exquisite cuisine.

At a Glance

  • Area: 79 sq km
  • Altitude: 123 m above sea level
  • Rainfall: 44 cms (June-Sept)
  • Best Time: October - March
  • Languages: Hindi, Urdu, English
  • STD Code: 0522

The Legend of Lakshmanpur

The history of Lucknow traces back to the ancient Suryavanshi Dynasty. It is said that Lakshmana, brother of Lord Rama, laid the foundation of the city near the Gomti River on an elevated piece of land called Lakshmanpur.

The city rose to prominence in the 18th Century when the Mughal emperors appointed Nawabs for administration. Under the powerful dynasty of the Nawabs, specifically Asaf-ud-Daula, Lucknow flourished as a center for poetry, dance, music, and a refined lifestyle.

Architectural Wonders

Bara Imambara

Built by Nawab Asafuddaula in 1784 as a famine relief project. It features the incredible Bhulbhuliya, a honeycombed maze of corridors.

Chota Imambara

Also known as the Husainabad Imambara, this structure is famous for its glittering brass domes and exquisite Belgian glass chandeliers.

Rumi Darwaza

A massive 60-foot-high door built with no wood or iron. It serves as the grand entrance to the Bara Imambara.

The Residency

The traditional home of British residents, it witnessed heavy cross-firing in 1857 and remains a significant historical site.

Parks, Culture & Kothis

  • Kothis: Discover the unique history of Kabooter wali Kothi (built for royal pigeons), Tara Kothi (an astronomical lab), and Bibiapur Kothi (a hunting lodge).

  • Gautam Buddha Park & Zoo: Popular recreation grounds featuring rides and the Prince of Wales Zoological Gardens, established in 1921.

  • Lucknow Mahautsav: A 10-day celebration (Nov 25 - Dec 5) showcasing Awadhi cuisine, Gazals, and Kathak recitals.

Plan Your Visit

By Air

Well connected by direct flights from major Indian cities.

By Rail

Lucknow Junction links to Delhi (Shatabdi), Mumbai (Pushpak), and Kolkata.

Shopping Hubs

Explore Hazratganj for high-end retail or Aminabad and Chowk for traditional Chikan work and Attar.
