Panna National Park

Land of the Wild Cats and India's Diamond Heart.

Essential Information

Area: 543 sq. km
Altitude: 212-538 meters
Best Time: January to May
Rainfall: 1100 mm
Temp: 13.5°C to 31.4°C
Language: Hindi, English

Overview

Panna National Park is situated in the central Indian state of Madhya Pradesh, just 57 km from the world-famous temples of Khajuraho. The Panna region is unique for its diamond industry and as a premier Tiger Reserve known worldwide for its wild cats, antelope, and deer population.

The park's proximity to Khajuraho makes it a must-visit destination for travelers looking to blend cultural heritage with India's best wildlife sightings.

History & Legend

Historically, this region served as the private hunting grounds for the rulers of Panna, Chhatarpur, and Bijawar. Local legends even claim that the Pandavas of the Mahabharata spent part of their seclusion within these very forests.

  • 1981: Formally declared as a National Park.
  • 1994: Designated as the 22nd Project Tiger Reserve in India.
  • Current Status: It is the 5th Tiger Reserve in the state of Madhya Pradesh.

Biodiversity

Wild Mammals

The park is home to the Tiger, Leopard, Sloth Bear, Wild Dog, Wolf, Hyena, and Caracal. The grasslands are frequently dotted with Blue Bull (Nilgai), Chinkara, and Spotted Deer.

Birdlife

Hosting over 200 species, including winter migrants. Highlights include the King Vulture, Paradise Flycatcher, and the National bird, the Peacock, especially captivating during monsoon dances.

Flora & Landscape

Characterized by extensive plateaus and gorges, the forest landscape includes the Upper Talgaon Plateau, Hinouta Plateau, and the Ken Valley. The vegetation is dominated by dry deciduous forests.

Travel Guide

By Air

The nearest airport is Khajuraho, located just 37 km from the park.

By Rail

The nearest railhead is Satna, at a distance of 90 km.

By Road

Panna bus stand is well-connected to all major cities in Madhya Pradesh.
