Pench National Park

A stunning landscape of diverse wildlife on the border of Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra.

Quick Facts

Area: 299 sq.km
Altitude: 200-1000m above sea level
Best Time: March to June
Annual Rainfall: 1300 mm
Temperature: 19.2 to 31.5°C
Language: Hindi, English

Overview

Situated on the border of Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra, Pench National Park is one of India's most accessible tiger reserves. The park derives its name from the Pench River, which flows through the sanctuary from north to south, dividing it into nearly equal eastern and western halves.

Declared a sanctuary in 1977, the area was elevated to National Park status in 1983 and officially established as a Tiger Reserve in 1992. It is celebrated for its abundant flora and fauna and its unique southern tropical dry deciduous teak forests.

Wildlife & Predators

Inhabitants

The park is famous for large herds of:

  • Gaur (Indian Bison)
  • Cheetal & Sambar
  • Nilgai & Wild Pig
  • Sloth Bear & Chinkara

The Predators

  • Royal Bengal Tiger
  • Leopard
  • Wild Dog (Dhole)
  • Wolf & Hyena

Avian Diversity

With over 285 species of resident and migratory birds, Pench is a paradise for birdwatchers.

Hornbill
Indian Pitta
Osprey
White-eyed Buzzard

In winter, thousands of migratory birds like Brahmini Ducks, Barheaded Geese, and Pochards visit the park's reservoir.

How to Reach

By Air

Sonegaon Airport at Nagpur is the nearest (93 km).

By Rail

Seoni Railway Station is the closest railhead (30 km).

By Road

Seoni is the nearest bus stand, well-connected to major hubs in MP and Maharashtra.

Flora

Deciduous teak and mixed forests containing rare herbs used for medicinal purposes.
