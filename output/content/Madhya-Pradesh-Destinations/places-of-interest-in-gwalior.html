Gwalior - Tour to Historical City

A district in Madhya Pradesh steeped in legend and architectural grandeur.

City History

The history of Gwalior is traced back to a legend in the 8th century AD when a chieftain named Suraj Sen was cured of a deadly disease by a hermit-saint named Gwalipa. In gratitude, Suraj Sen founded the city and named it in honor of the saint.

Places of Interest

Gwalior Fort

The most significant monument dominating the city, standing on a steep sandstone mass. It has witnessed momentous events, battles, and was described by Emperor Babur as "the pearl amongst the fortresses of Hind."

Gujari Mahal

Located within the fort, this medieval marvel has been converted into an Archaeological Museum housing rare antiquities dating back as far as the 1st century A.D.

Gopachal Parvat

Located on the slopes of Gwalior Fort, it features unique statues of Jain Tirthankars built by Tomar Kings. It includes the world's largest single-stone statue of Bhagwan Parshwanath on a lotus.

Jai Vilas Palace and Museum

The current residence of the Scindia family, featuring Italianate architecture. Over 25 rooms have been converted into the Jivaji Rao Scindia Museum to showcase a regal lifestyle.

Additional Highlights

  • Man Mandir Palace: Built by Raja Mansingh between 1486 and 1517.
  • Gurudwara Data Bandhi Chhod: Built on Gwalior Fort in memory of the 6th Sikh Guru, Guru Hargobind Saheb.
  • Sarod Ghar: A Museum of Music located in the ancestral house of Ustad Hafiz Ali Khan.
  • Ghaus Mohammed's Tomb: A notable historical tomb and tourist attraction.

How to Reach

By Rail

Well-linked with Delhi, Agra, Mumbai, and Chennai rail networks.

By Road

Connected with Agra, Mathura, Jaipur, Delhi, Lucknow, Bhopal, Indore, Jhansi, Khajuraho, and Ujjain.

By Air

Gwalior airport is 8 km away, connecting the city with Mumbai, Bhopal, Indore, and Delhi.
